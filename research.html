Current Research

CT Reconstruction, Representation Learning, and Predictive Imaging

My current research focuses on generative AI for thoracic CT, with an emphasis on reconstructing images across reconstruction domains while preserving anatomy, subtle texture, and quantitative radiomic information. My central framework is RA-CMF: Region-Adaptive Conditional MeanFlow, which combines global Conditional MeanFlow reconstruction with reinforcement-learning-driven regional refinement.

01 / Research Program

Reliable generative imaging before downstream prediction

A major challenge in quantitative CT analysis is that the same anatomy can appear differently because of reconstruction kernels, acquisition settings, scanner characteristics, and noise behavior. These technical differences can change subtle texture and quantitative measurements even when the underlying anatomy is unchanged.

My research approaches this problem in stages. First, I study how to reconstruct a source CT image into a target reconstruction domain while retaining patient-specific anatomy. Second, I study whether the reconstruction process itself can provide useful image representations. Third, I am interested in carrying those representations into patient-level predictive and multimodal models.

The goal is therefore broader than pixel similarity. A useful reconstruction should preserve clinically important structure, subtle texture, noise behavior, and radiomic consistency so the output remains reliable for quantitative analysis.

02 / Featured Framework

RA-CMF: Region-Adaptive Conditional MeanFlow for CT Image Reconstruction

RA-CMF is a two-stage reconstruction framework. The first stage performs a strong global reconstruction using a Conditional MeanFlow U-Net. The second stage uses an actor-critic reinforcement-learning controller to determine where additional local refinement is useful, how much refinement to allocate, and when the process should stop.

The motivation is that reconstruction difficulty is not spatially uniform. Much of a CT slice may already be reconstructed well after the global stage, while smaller regions around complex lung structure, lesions, tumor-adjacent tissue, or subtle texture may still benefit from additional correction. Applying the same refinement everywhere can waste computation and can disturb regions that are already stable.

RA-CMF therefore treats refinement as a spatial decision problem rather than a fixed post-processing operation. The MeanFlow backbone learns the overall source-to-target transformation, while the policy controller learns to allocate extra computation selectively.

03 / Data & Task

Patient-separated paired thoracic CT reconstruction

The main RA-CMF experiments use paired thoracic CT images so the model learns from two reconstructions of the same underlying anatomy rather than from unrelated scans.

For the paired reconstruction task, GE Standard is treated as the source domain and GE Lung as the target domain. Pairs are constructed within the same patient and imaging session with matched spatial position and compatible slice thickness, so the learning signal reflects reconstruction differences rather than anatomical mismatch.

Patient-level splitting

17 patients are used for training, 4 for validation, and 2 for testing. Slices from the same patient are never shared across splits.

Paired 2D reconstruction

The main experiments use approximately 15,000 training pairs, 3,000 validation pairs, and 1,500 test pairs. Individual slices are processed independently.

Pairing constraints

Pair construction uses the same patient and session, matched spatial position, and supported slice thicknesses of 1.25 mm or 2.5 mm.

Tumor ROI evaluation

A separate annotated tumor subset is used to evaluate whether reconstruction quality and quantitative radiomic reliability are preserved inside clinically important regions.

Why patient-level splitting matters

Neighboring CT slices from the same patient are highly correlated. For that reason, the train, validation, and test partitions are defined at the patient level even though the reconstruction model operates on individual 2D slices.

04 / Stage One

Conditional MeanFlow learns the global reconstruction trajectory

The first stage is not treated as a simple one-shot image-to-image mapping. The Conditional MeanFlow formulation models how an intermediate image state should move toward the target reconstruction domain.

The backbone is a Conditional MeanFlow U-Net. It receives the current intermediate state together with the source CT and predicts an image-sized average-velocity field. Time and reference-time variables are encoded through an RTEmbedding module and injected throughout the residual blocks, allowing the network to adapt its behavior across the trajectory.

Training combines MeanFlow consistency with image reconstruction supervision. Conditioning on the source CT anchors the transformation to the patient's observed anatomy rather than allowing unconstrained image synthesis.

  • Source conditioningThe source CT is available throughout the reconstruction process so the trajectory remains tied to patient-specific anatomy.
  • Intermediate statesThe model learns from states along a continuous reconstruction trajectory instead of only predicting a final output in a single direct mapping.
  • Velocity predictionThe U-Net predicts spatially resolved average-velocity information with the same image geometry as the CT slice.
  • Global roleThis stage performs the main reconstruction and produces a strong baseline image before regional refinement is considered.
05 / Stage Two

Reinforcement learning controls where extra refinement is worth the cost

The regional stage is built around the observation that some locations remain harder than others after global reconstruction. Instead of refining every region equally, the policy controller learns how to spend a limited refinement budget.

The controller observes information derived from the reconstruction rollout, including source and current images, difference and residual information, velocity-related information, the body mask, and the current step fraction. It then predicts spatial decisions and budget decisions through actor-critic heads.

The current design prioritizes subtle texture and local fidelity rather than aggressive edge sharpening. The aim is to make small targeted corrections where the target-domain difference remains meaningful while leaving already-correct anatomy stable.

Tile selection

The policy identifies spatial regions that should receive additional refinement based on the current reconstruction state.

Local step budget

Selected regions can receive different amounts of local micro-stepping instead of using a fixed number of updates everywhere.

Global budget

The controller limits total refinement effort across the slice so computation is not spent where the expected benefit is small.

Stopping and stability

Early stopping and inference safeguards help avoid unnecessary updates and reduce the chance of degrading regions that are already reconstructed well.

Why reinforcement learning?

The controller must jointly reason about where to refine, how much extra computation to allocate, whether another step is useful, and when to stop. This makes the problem naturally sequential rather than a single static segmentation decision.

06 / Evaluation

Evaluation goes beyond global pixel similarity

A reconstructed CT image can achieve good global similarity while still altering local texture or quantitative features. I therefore evaluate RA-CMF at multiple levels: whole-image quality, tumor-region quality, radiomic consistency, and noise behavior.

  • Full-image qualityPSNR and SSIM measure global reconstruction similarity across the complete CT slice.
  • Tumor ROI qualityROI-level PSNR and SSIM test whether reconstruction remains accurate in clinically important regions.
  • Radiomic reliabilityCCC is evaluated across first-order, GLCM, GLRLM, GLSZM, GLDM, and NGTDM radiomic feature families.
  • Noise behaviorNoise power spectrum analysis is used to examine whether reconstructed texture and noise characteristics move toward target-domain behavior.
07 / Updated Results

Updated reconstruction and radiomic performance

The updated evaluation shows that RA-CMF improves both ROI-level quantitative consistency and full-image reconstruction quality over the Conditional MeanFlow backbone.

08 / Ongoing Extension

Learning compact CT representations from the RA-CMF reconstruction process

My current work extends RA-CMF beyond reconstruction. The central question is whether internal features and region-selection behavior learned during reconstruction can be converted into a compact representation of a CT slice that is useful for similarity, retrieval, patient modeling, and downstream prediction.

The representation pipeline freezes the trained RA-CMF reconstruction system and trains a separate representation head. Features are collected from multiple levels of the MeanFlow U-Net and from different points along the reconstruction trajectory. Global slice information is combined with features from regions selected as informative by the regional controller.

A CLS-like learnable aggregation mechanism combines a global token with selected regional tokens into a compact embedding. The current implementation produces a 512-dimensional late-trajectory representation and uses a VICReg-style objective to align related trajectory views while preventing collapsed embeddings.

01 / Frozen reconstruction

Preserve RA-CMF behavior

The reconstruction backbone and controller remain frozen so representation learning does not change the already-trained reconstruction model.

02 / Global + regional tokens

Use informative spatial context

Global context is combined with selected local regions so the representation is not dominated only by whole-image averaging.

03 / Trajectory consistency

Align reconstruction views

Intermediate, late, and paired-target views provide related representations of the same anatomy for self-supervised alignment.

09 / Current Directions

From reconstructed slices to patient-level multimodal prediction

The broader direction of this work is to connect reconstruction, representation learning, and prediction rather than treating them as separate problems.

Patient-level CT representation

Aggregate information across multiple slices so the model can move from a single-slice embedding toward a representation of the complete patient CT study.

Multimodal modeling

Combine imaging representations with clinical context and other patient-level information to build richer predictive models.

Downstream prediction

Evaluate whether reconstructed images and learned representations improve risk, outcome, survival-related, and early-detection modeling rather than relying only on reconstruction metrics.

Generalization and safety

Extend validation across larger cohorts, additional acquisition settings and scanner vendors, and explicitly study whether subtle pathological findings remain unchanged after reconstruction.
