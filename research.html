Current Research

Generative CT Reconstruction to Patient-Level Prediction

My PhD research develops generative and predictive AI methods for thoracic CT. The central theme is to make imaging more reliable before asking a downstream model to make a clinical prediction. I first study reconstruction across CT reconstruction domains, then learn compact image representations from that reconstruction process, and finally use those representations for patient-level risk, outcome, and early-detection modeling.

01 / Research Program

One research program, three connected questions

CT-based AI can be affected by reconstruction kernels, scanner settings, noise characteristics, and acquisition differences. The same anatomy may therefore produce different texture and quantitative measurements even when the underlying disease state has not changed. My research asks how to reduce that technical variability without losing patient-specific anatomy, then how to use the resulting imaging signal for prediction.

AIM 01

CT reconstruction

Develop generative reconstruction methods that map a source CT reconstruction to a target reconstruction domain while preserving anatomy, subtle texture, noise behavior, and radiomic information. RA-CMF is the main framework for this aim.

AIM 02

Image representation + multimodal prediction

Learn compact CT representations from the reconstruction process, aggregate information across slices, and combine imaging with patient-level clinical information for downstream outcome and treatment-free-survival modeling.

AIM 03

Early detection on LDCT

Study whether reconstruction-aware imaging representations can support earlier lung cancer detection on low-dose CT, including prediction of clinically relevant events within a 12-month horizon.

02 / Featured Framework

RA-CMF: Region-Adaptive Conditional MeanFlow for CT Image Reconstruction

RA-CMF is a two-stage reconstruction framework designed around a simple observation: reconstruction difficulty is not uniform across a CT slice. A strong global model may already reconstruct most of the image well, while smaller regions containing complex lung structure, lesion boundaries, tumor-adjacent tissue, or subtle texture can still benefit from additional correction.

The first stage uses a Conditional MeanFlow U-Net to perform the global source-to-target reconstruction. The second stage treats local refinement as a sequential decision problem. A reinforcement-learning controller decides where additional refinement is needed, how much local computation to allocate, how much total budget to spend, and when refinement should stop.

The goal is not aggressive sharpening. The regional stage is designed for small, targeted corrections that improve difficult areas while leaving already-correct anatomy unchanged.

03 / Data & Experimental Design

Patient-separated paired thoracic CT reconstruction

The main RA-CMF study uses paired thoracic CT data from the National Lung Screening Trial collection available through TCIA. The reconstruction task is defined using two GE reconstruction domains from the same underlying anatomy: GE Standard as the source and GE Lung as the target.

Pair construction is performed within the same patient and imaging session. Spatial position is matched so the source and target slices represent the same anatomy, and supported slice thicknesses are restricted to 1.25 mm or 2.5 mm. Training and evaluation use a CT window of −800 to 600 HU.

Strict patient-level split

Slices from one patient are never shared across training, validation, and testing. This is important because neighboring CT slices are highly correlated and should not be treated as independent patient samples.

2D slice reconstruction

The main RA-CMF model operates on individual 2D slices. Across the primary split, the study contains approximately 19,500 paired slice samples, corresponding to roughly 39,000 individual source and target CT images.

Paired anatomy

Source and target images come from the same patient and session with matched spatial position, allowing the learning objective to reflect reconstruction-domain differences rather than anatomical mismatch.

Tumor-region analysis

A separate annotated tumor subset is used to evaluate whether reconstruction quality and quantitative radiomic information remain reliable inside clinically important regions.

04 / Stage One

Conditional MeanFlow models the global reconstruction trajectory

The first stage is not simply a one-shot image-to-image network. Conditional MeanFlow models the transformation dynamics between intermediate image states and the target reconstruction domain while conditioning on the observed source CT.

The 2D U-Net receives the current state together with source-image conditioning and time/reference-time information. It predicts an image-sized velocity field that describes how the current state should move along the reconstruction trajectory. Because the source CT remains available to the model, the transformation is anchored to the patient's observed anatomy rather than behaving like unconstrained image synthesis.

  • Source conditioningThe source CT remains part of the model input so patient-specific anatomy is retained throughout the transformation.
  • Intermediate trajectoryThe model learns how an image state should evolve toward the target rather than learning only a single direct source-to-target output.
  • Velocity fieldThe network predicts spatially resolved transformation information with the same geometry as the CT slice.
  • Global roleThis stage performs the primary reconstruction and provides the strong baseline that the region-adaptive stage selectively improves.
05 / Stage Two

Reinforcement learning allocates refinement where it is actually useful

After the global reconstruction, RA-CMF asks whether every part of the image should receive the same additional computation. The answer is usually no. The second stage therefore learns a policy for region selection, local refinement effort, total budget, and stopping.

The controller uses spatial and global heads to estimate refinement decisions. Selected regions receive local micro-steps through a soft-gated refinement process, while global budget and stopping decisions control how much additional work is performed over the complete slice. The policy is trained with an actor-critic PPO objective and a reward that balances reconstruction improvement against excessive compute and unnecessary regional updates.

My current emphasis in the refinement stage is subtle texture rather than edge enhancement. The desired behavior is a small, stable correction that makes difficult regions more target-like without introducing artificial structure.

Region selection

The policy estimates which spatial locations still justify additional reconstruction effort after the MeanFlow stage.

Local micro-step budget

Different regions can receive different amounts of local refinement rather than a fixed number of extra steps everywhere.

Global budget

The total amount of refinement is constrained so the method does not spend computation on regions where expected benefit is small.

Stopping & stability

Stopping decisions and inference safeguards reduce unnecessary updates and help prevent a refinement step from degrading an already-correct reconstruction.

Why this is different from fixed local refinement

RA-CMF does not assume that every difficult-looking patch should always be processed. The controller must jointly decide whether a region needs refinement, how much compute it deserves, whether another step is still beneficial, and whether the global refinement process should continue.

06 / Evaluation

Image fidelity, tumor-region quality, radiomics, and noise behavior

A reconstruction can look globally similar while still altering subtle texture or quantitative features. I therefore evaluate RA-CMF at several levels instead of relying on a single image-quality metric.

  • Full-image PSNR / SSIMMeasures overall reconstruction similarity across the complete CT slice.
  • Tumor ROI PSNR / SSIMMeasures local reconstruction fidelity inside clinically important tumor regions.
  • Radiomic CCCEvaluates reproducibility across first-order, GLCM, GLRLM, GLSZM, GLDM, and NGTDM radiomic feature families.
  • Noise power spectrumExamines whether reconstructed noise and texture characteristics move toward the target-domain behavior rather than only improving pixel error.

Pathology preservation

Because generative reconstruction can potentially alter subtle image content, ongoing evaluation also focuses on whether lesion appearance and clinically important local structure remain unchanged after reconstruction.

08 / Ongoing Extension

Learning CT representations from the RA-CMF reconstruction process

My current work extends RA-CMF beyond image reconstruction. The central question is whether the internal features, reconstruction trajectory, and region-selection behavior learned by RA-CMF can be converted into a compact representation of a CT slice that is useful for downstream modeling.

The current representation pipeline keeps the trained RA-CMF reconstruction system frozen and trains a separate representation head. Features are collected from multiple levels of the MeanFlow U-Net and from different points along the reconstruction trajectory. Global slice information is combined with controller-selected local regions so the representation includes both whole-image context and difficult spatial areas.

A CLS-like learnable aggregation mechanism combines one global token with selected regional tokens into a 512-dimensional slice embedding. A VICReg-style objective aligns related views of the same anatomy across reconstruction states and the paired target while discouraging collapsed representations.

02 / Global + local context

Use informative regional features

The late-trajectory representation is used as the main downstream embedding for similarity, patient modeling, and predictive tasks.

09 / Predictive Imaging

From slice embeddings to patient-level multimodal prediction

A single CT slice is not the final unit of clinical prediction. The next stage of my work studies how to aggregate reconstruction-aware slice representations into a patient-level CT representation and combine that imaging signal with clinical information.

Patient-level CT representation

Aggregate information across multiple slices so the model captures the complete study rather than treating a single 2D slice as the final patient representation.

Multimodal fusion

Combine imaging representations with clinical context and other patient-level information for richer risk and outcome models.

Treatment-free survival / outcome modeling

Evaluate whether reconstruction-aware CT representations improve downstream outcome prediction compared with raw-image or radiomics-only approaches.

Early detection

Study low-dose CT representations for detecting lung-cancer-related events earlier, including a 12-month predictive endpoint.

10 / Generalization & Validation

Moving beyond one reconstruction pair and one cohort

The broader validation program is focused on robustness across patients, acquisition settings, reconstruction protocols, scanner vendors, and downstream clinical tasks. These studies are important before making broad claims about clinical generalizability.

External CT cohorts

Additional public cohorts such as LIDC-IDRI and NSCLC Radiogenomics are being used or evaluated for representation, prediction, and generalization studies.

Reproducibility datasets

Paired/repeat imaging resources such as RIDER provide a way to study whether reconstruction improves quantitative repeatability rather than only visual similarity.

Phantom evaluation

GE and Siemens phantom datasets are part of the broader validation strategy for studying scanner- and protocol-related variation in a more controlled setting.

Clinical usefulness

Downstream classification, risk, survival, and early-detection experiments are used to test whether better reconstruction and representation quality translate into better predictive performance.
