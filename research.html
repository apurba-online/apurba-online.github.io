Current Research

CT Reconstruction, Representation Learning, and Predictive Imaging

My current research focuses on generative AI for thoracic CT, with an emphasis on reconstructing images across reconstruction domains while preserving anatomy, subtle texture, and quantitative radiomic information. My main framework is RA-CMF: Region-Adaptive Conditional MeanFlow, which combines a Conditional MeanFlow reconstruction backbone with reinforcement-learning-driven regional refinement.

01 / Research Program

Reliable generative imaging before downstream prediction

A major challenge in quantitative CT analysis is that the same anatomy can appear differently because of reconstruction kernels, acquisition settings, scanner characteristics, and noise behavior. These technical differences can alter subtle image texture and quantitative measurements even when the underlying anatomy is unchanged.

My research addresses this problem in stages. First, I study how to reconstruct a source CT image into a target reconstruction domain while preserving patient-specific anatomy. Second, I study whether the learned reconstruction process can provide useful image representations. Third, I am interested in using those representations for patient-level predictive modeling and multimodal analysis.

The goal is not simply to maximize pixel similarity. The reconstructed image should preserve clinically important structure, subtle texture, noise behavior, and radiomic consistency so it remains useful for quantitative analysis.

02 / Featured Framework

RA-CMF: Region-Adaptive Conditional MeanFlow for CT Image Reconstruction

RA-CMF is a two-stage reconstruction framework. The first stage performs a strong global reconstruction using a Conditional MeanFlow U-Net. The second stage uses a reinforcement-learning controller to decide where additional local refinement is useful, how much refinement should be applied, and when the process should stop.

Reconstruction difficulty is not spatially uniform. Many parts of a CT slice may already be reconstructed well after the global stage, while smaller regions around complex lung structure, lesion boundaries, tumor-adjacent tissue, or subtle texture may still benefit from additional correction. Applying the same iterative refinement everywhere can waste computation and may disturb regions that are already correct.

RA-CMF therefore treats refinement as a spatial decision problem rather than a fixed post-processing step. The MeanFlow backbone learns the overall source-to-target transformation, while the policy controller learns how to allocate additional computation selectively.

03 / Data & Task

Patient-separated paired thoracic CT reconstruction

The main RA-CMF experiments use paired thoracic CT images so the model learns from different reconstructions of the same underlying anatomy rather than unrelated scans.

In the paired reconstruction task, GE Standard is treated as the source domain and GE Lung as the target domain. Pair construction is performed within the same patient and imaging session, with matched spatial position and compatible slice thickness so the learning signal reflects reconstruction differences rather than anatomical mismatch.

Patient-level splitting

17 patients are used for training, 4 for validation, and 2 for testing. Slices from the same patient are never shared across different data splits.

Paired 2D reconstruction

The main experiments use approximately 15,000 training pairs, 3,000 validation pairs, and 1,500 test pairs. Each 2D slice is processed independently by the reconstruction model.

Pairing constraints

Pairs are formed from the same patient and session, with matched spatial position and supported slice thicknesses of 1.25 mm or 2.5 mm.

Tumor ROI evaluation

A separate annotated tumor subset is used to test whether reconstruction quality and radiomic reliability are preserved within clinically important regions.

Why the patient-level split matters

Neighboring CT slices from the same patient are highly correlated. For this reason, the train, validation, and test partitions are defined at the patient level even though the reconstruction model itself operates on individual slices.

04 / Stage One

Conditional MeanFlow learns the global reconstruction trajectory

The first stage is not simply a direct one-shot image-to-image mapping. RA-CMF uses a Conditional MeanFlow formulation to model transformation dynamics between intermediate image states and the target reconstruction domain.

The backbone is a 2D Conditional MeanFlow U-Net. It receives the current state together with conditioning information from the source CT and time/reference-time embedding, then predicts an image-sized velocity field describing how the current image state should move through the reconstruction trajectory.

Conditioning on the source CT anchors the generative transformation to the observed anatomy. The MeanFlow stage therefore performs the main global reconstruction before any region-specific refinement is requested.

  • Conditional inputThe source CT remains available to the network so the reconstruction trajectory stays tied to the observed patient anatomy.
  • Intermediate statesThe model reasons about states along the reconstruction trajectory rather than learning only a single direct source-to-target output.
  • Velocity predictionThe U-Net predicts spatially resolved velocity information with the same geometry as the CT slice.
  • Global roleThis stage performs the main reconstruction and provides a strong baseline output before regional refinement.
05 / Stage Two

Reinforcement learning controls where refinement is worth the cost

The regional stage is based on the observation that some locations remain more difficult than others after the global reconstruction. Instead of refining every location equally, a policy controller learns how to spend a limited refinement budget.

The controller observes a state built from reconstruction-related feature maps, including the source image, current state, difference information, local change, velocity, body mask, step fraction, and residual error. Spatial heads estimate where to refine and how many local micro-steps to allocate, while global heads control overall budget and early stopping.

The reward balances reconstruction quality and localized improvement against compute cost and excessive usage. The design emphasizes subtle texture and stable local correction rather than aggressive sharpening.

Tile selection

The policy identifies spatial regions that should receive additional refinement based on the current reconstruction state and rollout information.

Local micro-step budget

Selected regions can receive different amounts of refinement rather than using a single fixed number of local updates everywhere.

Global budget

The policy limits total refinement effort across the slice so computation is not spent where expected benefit is small.

Stopping & stability

Early stopping and inference safeguards are used to avoid unnecessary updates and reject changes that reduce reconstruction quality.

Why reinforcement learning?

The decision is not only “which patch looks different?” The controller must jointly reason about region selection, local refinement amount, global computation budget, expected improvement, and when to stop. Reinforcement learning provides a natural framework for optimizing these sequential tradeoffs.

06 / Evaluation

Evaluation goes beyond global pixel similarity

A reconstructed CT image can achieve good global similarity while still altering subtle local texture or quantitative features. I therefore evaluate RA-CMF at multiple levels: whole-image quality, tumor-region quality, radiomic consistency, and noise behavior.

  • Full-image qualityPSNR and SSIM measure global reconstruction similarity across the complete CT slice.
  • Tumor ROI qualityROI-level PSNR and SSIM evaluate whether reconstruction remains accurate in clinically important regions rather than only in large homogeneous areas.
  • Radiomic reliabilityConcordance correlation coefficient (CCC) is evaluated across first-order, GLCM, GLRLM, GLSZM, GLDM, and NGTDM radiomic feature families.
  • Noise behaviorNoise power spectrum analysis is used to examine whether reconstructed texture and noise characteristics move toward target-domain behavior.

08 / Updated Results

Updated quantitative reconstruction and radiomic performance

The updated evaluation shows that RA-CMF improves both ROI-level quantitative consistency and full-image reconstruction quality over the Conditional MeanFlow backbone.

In the current result set, RA-CMF reaches an ROI CCC of 0.93 ± 0.09, ROI PSNR of 31.94 ± 2.64, ROI SSIM of 0.97 ± 0.03, full-image PSNR of 34.23 ± 1.71, and full-image SSIM of 0.95 ± 0.01.

09 / Ongoing Extension

Learning compact CT representations from the RA-CMF reconstruction process

My current work extends RA-CMF beyond reconstruction. The central question is whether internal features and region-selection behavior learned during reconstruction can be converted into a compact representation of a CT slice that is useful for similarity, retrieval, patient modeling, and downstream prediction.

The current representation pipeline freezes the trained RA-CMF reconstruction system and trains a separate representation head. Features are collected from multiple levels of the MeanFlow U-Net and from different points along the reconstruction trajectory. Global slice information is combined with features from regions selected as informative by the regional controller.

A CLS-like learnable aggregation mechanism combines one global token with selected regional tokens into a compact embedding. The current implementation produces a 512-dimensional late-trajectory representation and uses a VICReg-style objective to align representations across intermediate trajectory states and the paired target reconstruction while avoiding collapsed embeddings.

01 / Frozen reconstruction

Preserve RA-CMF behavior

The reconstruction backbone and controller remain frozen so representation learning does not change the already-trained reconstruction model.

02 / Global + regional tokens

Use what the controller finds important

Global context is combined with selected local regions so the embedding is not dominated only by whole-image averaging.

03 / Trajectory consistency

Align multiple reconstruction views

Intermediate, late, and paired-target views provide related representations of the same anatomy for self-supervised alignment.

What the embedding represents

The learned vector is not intended to be a manually interpretable list of clinical measurements. It is a learned fingerprint of the CT slice designed to summarize information distributed across the reconstruction trajectory and spatial regions for downstream models.

10 / Current Directions

From reconstructed slices to patient-level multimodal prediction

The broader direction of this work is to connect reconstruction, representation learning, and prediction rather than treating them as separate tasks.

Patient-level CT representation

Aggregate information across multiple slices so the model can move from a single-slice embedding toward a representation of the complete patient CT study.

Multimodal modeling

Combine imaging representations with clinical context and other patient-level information to build richer predictive models.

Downstream prediction

Evaluate whether reconstructed images and learned imaging representations improve risk, outcome, survival-related, and early-detection modeling rather than relying only on reconstruction metrics.

Generalization & safety

Extend validation across larger cohorts, additional acquisition settings and scanner vendors, and explicitly study whether subtle pathological findings remain unchanged after reconstruction.
